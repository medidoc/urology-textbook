Assessment of tumor stage and biological aggressiveness is crucial for treatment decisions in prostate cancer. Diagnostic evaluation includes digital rectal examination (DRE), PSA concentration, prostate biopsy histology, laboratory tests, imaging, and, in selected cases, pelvic lymphadenectomy.

Clinical Staging of Prostate Cancer

Digital rectal examination, PSA concentration, and Gleason grade each correlate well with the pathological tumor stage. However, the predictive accuracy of an individual parameter is too low for a given patient. Combining these variables allows reliable estimation of clinical risk (prostate cancer mortality) [Table D’Amico risk classification].

The Partin tables combine local tumor stage, PSA concentration, and Gleason score to estimate the risk of extraprostatic extension, seminal vesicle invasion, and lymph node metastases. Some authors consider that the tables underestimate lymph node metastasis risk because of the limited lymphadenectomy template used in the clinical series.

The Kattan nomograms of the Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center likewise combine local tumor stage, PSA concentration, and Gleason score. They estimate 10-year progression-free survival after radical prostatectomy and 15-year cancer-specific survival.

Bone Scintigraphy

Bone scintigraphy is a conventional staging modality for bone metastases with relatively high sensitivity but limited specificity [Figure bone metastases in prostate cancer]. Indeterminate findings should be evaluated by MRI, CT, or conventional radiography according to their location and the clinical question; bone biopsy should be considered when uncertainty remains.

Bone scintigraphy should be omitted in patients with a PSA concentration below 10 ng/ml, Gleason score below 8, clinically localized disease, and no bone pain.

Computed Tomography

CT is indicated in advanced prostate cancer, either in combination with bone scintigraphy or as part of a PET/CT examination. CT can detect enlarged lymph nodes, bone metastases, hydronephrosis, and infiltration of adjacent organs. In asymptomatic patients with a PSA concentration below 20 ng/ml and a Gleason score below 8, treatment-relevant pathological findings are uncommon.

Positron Emission Tomography

The main indications for PSMA-PET/CT are high-risk prostate cancer, biochemical recurrence after curative treatment, and progression during systemic therapy for metastatic prostate cancer. PET combined with CT or MRI provides very high diagnostic accuracy and is superior to the combination of bone scintigraphy and CT (Bukavina et al., 2023). Tracers include 68Ga-PSMA and 18F-labeled PSMA ligands.

Multiparametric MRI of the Prostate

Multiparametric MRI of the prostate is indicated for imaging locally advanced prostate cancer when imaging findings may alter the treatment decision [Figure multiparametric MRI in pT3b prostate cancer].




Pelvic Lymphadenectomy

Pelvic lymphadenectomy is a surgical staging procedure when there is a relevant probability of lymph node metastases and is usually performed during radical prostatectomy. Historically, isolated surgical lymph node staging was discussed before perineal prostatectomy or brachytherapy; it is no longer routinely indicated. Indications and extent are discussed in the section on radical prostatectomy. See also laparoscopic pelvic lymphadenectomy for technique and complications.

Experimental Diagnostic Methods

New Screening or Tumor Markers

PCA3:

PCA3 (prostate cancer antigen 3) is a non-protein-coding RNA that is overexpressed in prostate cancer and can be detected in urine after prostatic massage. PCA3 may provide additional information in selected patients with an elevated PSA concentration and a previous negative biopsy, but it is not a standard test for population-based early detection.

Analysis of Gene Expression:

Tissue-based gene-expression and genomic-classifier tests, such as Prolaris, Decipher Prostate, or the Genomic Prostate Score, may provide additional prognostic information in selected patients with localized prostate cancer. They are used mainly to estimate tumor aggressiveness and to support treatment decisions, for example active surveillance versus curative treatment. Routine use is not recommended.

Other Methods:

Other experimental approaches include detection of micrometastases in bone marrow and RT-PCR for detecting prostate cancer cells in blood or lymph nodes.

Imaging

New methods, including PET, MRI with specialized contrast agents, and specialized ultrasound techniques, are being evaluated in clinical series.

Micro-ultrasound:

Targeted prostate biopsy using a high-resolution TRUS probe (ExactVu, 29 MHz) may support detection of clinically significant prostate cancer (Dariane et al., 2022). The method is promising but does not currently replace guideline-based mpMRI-guided and systematic diagnostic evaluation.

Elastography:

Ultrasound elastography displays the elasticity of prostate tissue in color by applying pressure with an ultrasound probe. Less elastic regions are suspicious for cancer. However, sensitivity and specificity are insufficient to omit systematic or mpMRI-targeted prostate biopsy.

Analysis of Ultrasonography Raw Data:

Database-assisted analysis of ultrasound raw data, such as HistoScanning, was developed to improve prostate cancer detection. Independent studies have not demonstrated sufficient sensitivity and specificity for routine clinical use.
