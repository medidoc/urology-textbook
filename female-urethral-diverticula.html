Diverticula of the Female Urethra: Diagnosis and Treatment

Definition of Female Urethral Diverticula

Diverticula of the female urethra usually originate from periurethral glands in the distal or mid urethra and expand into the space between the endopelvic and periurethral fascia (Aspera et al., 2002) (Rufford and Cardozo, 2004).

Epidemiology:

Female urethral diverticula are rare, with a prevalence of approximately 0.02–0.1 %.


Etiology of Female Urethral Diverticula

Acquired Diverticula of the Female Urethra

Female urethral diverticula usually arise from inflamed periurethral glands. The diverticular opening is often located posterolaterally in the distal or mid urethra. Recurrent inflammation and obstruction of the opening lead to enlargement of the diverticulum within the endopelvic and periurethral fascia. The fascial boundaries may cause the diverticulum to extend circumferentially around the urethra. Diverticula range in size from a few millimeters to several centimeters.

Congenital Diverticula of the Female Urethra

Congenital diverticula are rare. Spontaneous regression has been described in case reports.

Signs and Symptoms of Urethral Diverticula

  • Irritative symptoms, such as urinary frequency and dysuria
  • Obstructive symptoms, such as a weak urinary stream
  • Dyspareunia
  • Recurrent urinary tract infections
  • Postvoid dribbling
  • A palpable vaginal mass

Complications:

Abscess, diverticular calculi, and carcinoma arising in a diverticulum (rare).

Diagnostic Workup

  • Urethrocystoscopy and vaginal examination: Basic examinations for differential diagnosis; the diverticular opening can often be visualized.
  • MRI: Establishes the diagnosis and delineates the anatomy of the diverticulum.
  • Double-balloon urethrography and VCUG: Optional imaging methods that can show the diverticulum radiographically in a lateral projection. They have largely been superseded by MRI. A special catheter is placed in the urethra, which is sealed by two balloons. Contrast medium is injected through a separate channel to visualize the diverticulum.

Treatment of Female Urethral Diverticula

Conservative Observation

Conservative observation is appropriate for small, asymptomatic diverticula.

Transvaginal Excision

Transvaginal excision is the treatment of choice for symptomatic or large female urethral diverticula.

  • Exposure of the diverticulum through a U-shaped incision in the vaginal wall
  • Opening of the periurethral fascia and excision of the diverticulum at the level of the urethra
  • Longitudinal closure of the urethra
  • Transverse closure of the periurethral fascia
  • Closure of the vagina
