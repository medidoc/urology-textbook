Meatal Stenosis and Stricture of the Female Urethra

Definition

Narrowing of the female urethra usually affects the distal portion and less often the proximal portion. An anatomically fixed narrowing of < 14 Ch is considered significant (Osman et al., 2013). Normal urethral caliber is more than 20 Ch. In girls, the expected urethral caliber (in Charrière) can be estimated using the following rule of thumb:

Urethral caliber [Charrière] = age + 10

Signs and Symptoms

Irritative urinary symptoms and recurrent urinary tract infections are often associated with urethral narrowing. The pathophysiological significance of a urethral stricture in the absence of residual urine or urinary retention is controversial.

Diagnostic Workup

Cystoscopy and urethral calibration with a bougie à boule are key examinations. If these procedures are not tolerated under local anesthesia, they are performed under general anesthesia. The role of urodynamic studies to confirm subvesical obstruction remains controversial.

Treatment

The indication for surgery should be assessed very carefully.

Distal meatal stenosis:

The fibrotic ring is enlarged by a sagittal incision at the 6 o'clock position. The ring tightens during calibration, and the wound edges are then approximated transversely.

Mid and proximal urethral strictures:

Internal urethrotomy (Otis procedure) is an option for mid and proximal strictures, but recurrence rates are high in true fibrotic strictures. For recurrent strictures, open urethroplasty using a ventral or dorsal buccal mucosa graft is the preferred approach.
