Causes, Diagnosis and Treatment of Ureteritis Cystica

Definition:

Ureteritis cystica is a rare benign inflammatory reaction of the ureteral urothelium characterized by multiple subepithelial cysts and vesicle-like mucosal elevations. See also cystitis cystica.


Ureteroscopic findings of ureteritis cystica: small subepithelial vesicles of the ureteral mucosa.

Etiology and pathology:

Ureteritis cystica is a chronic reactive change in the urothelium, usually in response to infection or mechanical irritation, as with nephrolithiasis. It produces subepithelial vesicles a few millimeters in size. Histologically, subepithelial urothelial cysts with a chronic inflammatory infiltrate are seen.

Signs and symptoms:

Ureteritis cystica is usually asymptomatic. Possible manifestations include hematuria, urinary obstruction with flank pain, symptoms of a urinary tract infection, or symptoms of nephrolithiasis.

Diagnosis:

Imaging with CT urography, intravenous urography, or retrograde pyelography shows multiple small, round, smoothly marginated protrusions of the ureteral wall into the lumen [fig.].

Ureteroscopy:

Ureteroscopy shows multiple small, yellowish or translucent subepithelial cysts. Biopsy may be performed to confirm the diagnosis if needed.

Retrograde pyelography of a patient with ureteritis cystica: multiple small, round, smoothly marginated filling defects projecting into the ureteral lumen.

Differential diagnosis:

Urothelial carcinoma, blood clots, calculi, polyps, sloughed papillae, and ureteritis follicularis.

Therapy:

Ureteritis cystica is benign and does not itself require treatment. Underlying causes and associated conditions, such as urinary tract infection or nephrolithiasis, should be treated. For significant obstruction, management may include urinary drainage with a double-J (DJ) ureteral stent, stone treatment, or, rarely, endoscopic treatment of the obstructing lesions.
