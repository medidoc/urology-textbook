Classification and Diagnosis of Urinary Incontinence

Classification of Urinary Incontinence

Definitions

The International Continence Society (ICS) defines urinary incontinence as a complaint of involuntary loss of urine.

Stress urinary incontinence (SUI):

Stress urinary incontinence occurs when bladder pressure exceeds urethral closure pressure during a passive increase in pressure, usually caused by increased abdominal pressure. Its leading symptom is involuntary urine loss during physical exertion, sneezing, or coughing.

Urgency urinary incontinence (UUI):

Urgency urinary incontinence is involuntary urine loss due to an active increase in bladder pressure, usually caused by an uninhibited detrusor contraction or detrusor overactivity. The leading symptom is involuntary urine loss accompanying or occurring immediately after urgency.

Mixed or situational urinary incontinence:

Any combination of stress urinary incontinence and urgency urinary incontinence is possible. Urinary incontinence in specific situations is also often multifactorial.

Giggle urinary incontinence:

Laughter triggers bladder emptying; this form occurs predominantly in children (enuresis risoria).

Coital urinary incontinence:

Situational urinary incontinence during sexual intercourse or orgasm.

Chronic urinary retention:

With urinary retention, particularly chronic retention, bladder pressure rises above urethral closure pressure. The full, overdistended bladder leaks urine without detrusor contractions (formerly called overflow incontinence).

Enuresis:

Intermittent incontinence during periods of sleep. Further subdivisions and definitions of enuresis are provided in the corresponding section.

Extraurethral incontinence:

Urine leakage through channels other than the urethral meatus, such as a vesicovaginal fistula or a urethrovaginal fistula, or a malformation such as an ectopic ureter.

Epidemiology

Prevalence of urinary incontinence:

The prevalence is 5–20 % in women and 3–10 % in men, depending on age and comorbidity.

General Diagnostic Workup

History:

A thorough medical history helps distinguish between forms of urinary incontinence. Important factors include the extent of urinary incontinence (daily pad use), degree of distress, previous operations, vaginal deliveries, medications (alpha blockers, clonidine), and neurologic and urologic diseases.

Quantification of stress urinary incontinence:

A micturition diary records fluid intake, voided volumes, incontinence episodes, and pad use. A period of 24–48 h is usually sufficient.

Laboratory tests:

Urine sediment and urine culture. Serum creatinine is measured to assess kidney function.

Physical examination:

In addition to an orienting neurological examination, a vaginal and rectal examination is performed in the lithotomy position. Important abnormal findings include cystocele or rectocele, pelvic organ prolapse assessed according to the ICS, decreased anal sphincter tone, absent anal reflex, clitoral or bulbocavernosus reflex, and demonstration of incontinence during straining, coughing, or the heel impact test.

Ultrasound imaging:

Ultrasound imaging is used to assess postvoid residual volume, hydronephrosis, renal parenchymal thickness, and bladder wall thickness.

Voiding cystourethrography:

Voiding cystourethrography (VCUG) is usually performed in combination with urodynamics. It is used in the differential diagnosis of complex forms of urinary incontinence, particularly to detect extraurethral incontinence, cystocele, a hypermobile urethra, and/or opening of the bladder neck during straining. In women, the study is performed in the lateral projection.

Urodynamics:

Urodynamics is indicated when the diagnosis is unclear, in complex cases, with neurologic comorbidities or elevated postvoid residual volume, and before invasive treatment. It can best identify the cause(s) of urinary incontinence (stress, urgency, or extraurethral incontinence).

Cystoscopy:

Cystoscopy is used in the differential diagnosis when intravesical pathology, fistula, foreign material, hematuria, or recurrent infection is suspected.
