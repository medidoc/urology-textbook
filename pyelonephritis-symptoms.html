Acute Pyelonephritis: Signs, Symptoms and Diagnosis

  • Acute pyelonephritis: definition, causes and pathology
  • Acute pyelonephritis: symptoms and diagnostic workup
  • Acute pyelonephritis: treatment

Guidelines: EAU Guidelines Urological Infections, (German S3 Guidelines UTI).

Signs and Symptoms of Acute Pyelonephritis

  • Sudden fever and chills, malaise, and weakness
  • Constant flank pain with costovertebral angle tenderness; children may describe the pain as abdominal
  • Frequency and dysuria, although these symptoms may be absent
  • Possible symptoms include nausea and vomiting, diarrhea, marked malaise, abdominal tenderness, guarding, rebound tenderness, and decreased bowel sounds
  • Tachycardia, hypotension, decreased alertness, and other signs of sepsis

Complications

  • Renal scarring, especially in children, with possible development of chronic pyelonephritis
  • Renal or perirenal abscess
  • Sepsis and septic shock
  • Emphysematous pyelonephritis in patients with diabetes mellitus, which has high mortality
  • Renal parenchymal damage due to scarring

Diagnosis of Acute Pyelonephritis

The diagnosis is based primarily on the triad of fever, flank pain, and pyuria. Radiologic findings are subtle and nonspecific and are present in only one in four patients. Imaging is useful for differential diagnosis, identifying associated or underlying conditions, and detecting complications (Dalla-Palma and Pozzi-Mucelli, 2000; Kawashima et al., 2000).

Blood Tests

  • Leukocytosis
  • Elevated ESR and elevated CRP
  • Blood cultures in patients with severe disease

Urine Tests

  • Cloudy urine, pyuria (granulocytes), and white blood cell casts
  • Bacteriuria
  • Mild proteinuria
  • Often microscopic hematuria

Urine Culture

Obtain a urine culture before starting antibiotic treatment to identify the pathogen and determine its antimicrobial susceptibility.

Ultrasonography of the Kidneys

Renal ultrasonography is the initial imaging study to exclude complicating factors, particularly urinary tract obstruction, urinary stones, abscess, or anatomical abnormalities. Sonographic findings of pyelonephritis are nonspecific and should be interpreted only in comparison with prior imaging: renal enlargement and a hypoechoic, thickened parenchymal mantle.

In emphysematous pyelonephritis, gas appears as echogenic structures with posterior acoustic shadowing and can be detected in the renal parenchyma and perirenal tissues. Renal abscesses appear as hypoechoic masses and may contain gas.

Computed Tomography

CT is indicated if the patient remains febrile after 48–72 hours or if a renal or perirenal abscess, nephrolithiasis, emphysematous nephritis, or obstruction is suspected. CT reliably detects these complications. Findings in uncomplicated acute pyelonephritis are subtle.

Compared with the contralateral kidney, the affected kidney may be enlarged, with wedge-shaped areas of decreased contrast enhancement and a delayed nephrogram. Perirenal inflammatory infiltrates and reduced function of the affected kidney may also be present.

Intravenous Urography

Intravenous urography no longer has a routine role in the modern diagnosis of acute pyelonephritis and has largely been replaced by ultrasonography and CT.

Findings in uncomplicated acute pyelonephritis are subtle and nonspecific: unilateral renal enlargement, delayed contrast enhancement of the affected kidney, and narrowed, mildly splayed calyces due to parenchymal swelling. In destructive pyelonephritis, the papillary tips may appear irregular, and papillary necrosis may be present. In emphysematous pyelonephritis, gas may be visible within the renal fascia.

Excluding Vesicoureteral Reflux

Recurrent episodes of pyelonephritis warrant evaluation for vesicoureteral reflux, particularly in children. The diagnostic workup depends on age, ultrasound findings, recurrence frequency, and local practice.

Differential Diagnosis

  • Urolithiasis with colic or obstruction
  • Renal or perirenal abscess
  • Renal infarction
  • Pancreatitis
  • Basal pneumonia
  • Pleuritis
  • Acute appendicitis
  • Acute cholecystitis
  • Sigmoid diverticulitis
  • Adnexitis
