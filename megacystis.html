Fetal and Congenital Megacystis

Definition:

Fetal or congenital megacystis is a pathological enlargement of the bladder with various etiologies. In the first trimester, a longitudinal bladder dimension greater than 7 mm is considered megacystis. Later in pregnancy, a sagittal bladder diameter greater than gestational age (in weeks) + 12 mm is suggestive of megacystis (Taghavi et al., 2017).

Epidemiology:

The prevalence is approximately 1:1500 in the first trimester.

Etiology:

Many syndromes and diseases can cause megacystis:

  • With lower urinary tract obstruction (LUTO): Posterior urethral valves (the most common cause), urethral stenosis, or atresia.
  • Vesicoureteral reflux: Reflux of urine between the bladder and ureters can gradually increase bladder capacity and ureteral diameter. Because not every patient with vesicoureteral reflux develops megacystis, additional factors related to bladder and ureteral function play a role.
  • Megacystis with microcolon-intestinal hypoperistalsis syndrome (MMIHS) (Berdon syndrome). MMIHS is a form of familial visceral myopathy. Other manifestations include megaduodenum and megacystis-megaureter syndrome.
  • Prune-belly syndrome, trisomies 13, 18, or 21, and caudal regression syndrome.

Prognosis:

Oligohydramnios, lung hypoplasia, renal dysfunction, and intestinal obstruction are associated with a poor prognosis.

Treatment:

Treatment depends on the underlying cause. See treatment of lower urinary tract obstruction, vesicoureteral reflux, and posterior urethral valves.
