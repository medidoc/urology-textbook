Bladder Tamponade: Diagnosis and Treatment

Definition

Bladder tamponade is the filling of the urinary bladder with blood clots due to severe hematuria, causing impaired voiding and potentially acute urinary retention.


Intravenous urogram showing bladder tamponade: a large, rounded filling defect in the bladder. With kind permission, Prof. Dr. R. Harzmann, Augsburg.

Etiology of Bladder Tamponade

  • Iatrogenic: Bladder tamponade often occurs after transurethral resection of the bladder (TURB) or prostate (TURP).
  • Risk factors: Platelet dysfunction and therapeutic anticoagulation.
  • Other causes: Tumors of the kidney, bladder, or prostate; benign prostatic hyperplasia; hemorrhagic cystitis; vascular diseases; and other causes of hematuria.

Signs and Symptoms

Typical symptoms include severe lower abdominal pain due to acute bladder distension, painful urinary retention, and gross hematuria with passage of blood clots. Secondary upper urinary tract obstruction may occur. Bladder perforation is a risk after deep TURB due to thinning of the bladder wall; it is unlikely when the bladder wall is intact.

Diagnosis

  • Ultrasound shows a variably distended bladder containing echogenic, sometimes mobile clots [fig. US of bladder tamponade].
  • Laboratory evaluation includes a complete blood count and coagulation studies; obtain a type and crossmatch as needed.
  • Further imaging is unnecessary for postoperative bleeding [fig. IVP of bladder tamponade]. For a first presentation, evaluate the hematuria according to the diagnostic workup of hematuria.

Ultrasound image of bladder tamponade (sagittal plane): a round echogenic lesion in the distended bladder.

Treatment of Bladder Tamponade

Fresh or mild bladder tamponades can be evacuated by aspiration with a bladder syringe through a catheter, preferably a sturdy straight catheter or irrigation catheter of 20–24 CH. This painful procedure requires adequate analgesia. After evacuation, insert an irrigation catheter and begin continuous bladder irrigation according to the severity of the hematuria.

For treatment-refractory or pronounced tamponade with suspected active bleeding, endoscopic intervention under general anesthesia is indicated. Evacuate the clots through the resectoscope sheath and then coagulate the bleeding source.
